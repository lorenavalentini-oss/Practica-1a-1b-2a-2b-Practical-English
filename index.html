1. Meet the verb TO BE ✨

TO BE means ser / estar. In English, the form changes with the subject.

💙 AM

I am 14.
I am Argentinian.
I'm

💗 IS

He / She / It is happy.
My sister is 10.
He's / She's / It's

💚 ARE

You / We / They are friends.
They are Brazilian.
You're / We're / They're
Affirmative: She is Spanish.
Negative: She isn't Spanish. (is not)
Question: Is she Spanish? → Yes, she is. / No, she isn't.
Remember: I am not → I'm not; are not → aren't.
Question order: AM / IS / ARE + person + information + ?
Are you from Chile? · Is your brother 12? · Where are they from?

6. Family connections 👨‍👩‍👧‍👦

Parents
mother + father
Siblings
brother + sister
Grandparents
grandmother + grandfather
Other relatives
aunt · uncle · cousin
Possessive 's: Emma's mother = the mother of Emma.
My mother's brother is my uncle.

7. Read & talk 🎭

Mia: Hi! I'm Mia. What's your name?

Sofia: I'm Sofia. Are you from Argentina?

Mia: No, I'm not. I'm from Chile. I'm Chilean. What about you?

Sofia: I'm Argentinian. This is my cousin, Leo.

Mia: Is he 14?

Sofia: No, he isn't. He's 12. His parents are from Brazil.

Mia: Are they Brazilian?

Sofia: Yes, they are!

🗣️ Pair speaking

Work in pairs. Change the names, ages, countries and relatives. Act out the conversation.

Ask your partner: What's your name? · How old are you? · Where are you from? · What's your nationality? · Have you got brothers or sisters? · Is your best friend 14? · Where are your grandparents from?
